Marble Ink · 013 · Gallery 100
I

Water Holds Its Breath

Before the ink falls, the water holds its breath. A single drop of indigo breaks the surface and refuses to decide — it spreads like a rumor, thin and certain. Marbling is the art of asking color to move on its own, and then leaving it alone enough to believe it.

II

The Comb Drags Color Through Color

A comb drawn across the tray writes veins into the surface — red pulled through gold, gold folded back over red. Every line is a decision made by hand, by gravity, by the slant of the room. Scroll, and the field turns; the same inks, the same water, a different weather.

III

Shear, Then Stillness

Move the paper and the pattern remembers where it was. Shear is just memory with a slant: strokes that began as rivers keep their current while the sheet beneath them rotates. What you see at the top of the scroll will never appear here — the water only moves forward.

IV

No Two Sheets Agree

At the end the film is lifted, cut, and pressed onto paper. The ink dries into stone — a marbled slab of three colors that could not exist a second ago and will not exist a second after. Keep scrolling back; the canvas only ever adds. Like the water, it keeps its breath.
